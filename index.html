01 / The count

How your vote
actually counts

New Zealand runs on MMP: two votes, 120 seats, one threshold. It is the most consequential arithmetic in the country, and almost nobody explains it at the door. So we built the machine. Take the dial, or keep reading and it will walk you through it.

Live from the machine

14/ 120

12 percent is 14 seats. That leaves 47 short of the 61 needed to govern, so the next question is who we govern with.

  1. 01

    Your party vote builds the House

    The party vote decides how many of the 120 seats each party gets. Not the local race, not the leader, not the last three weeks of advertising. The party vote, on its own, sets the shape of Parliament.

  2. 02

    Your electorate vote picks a local MP

    Your second vote chooses the person who holds your electorate seat. Win an electorate and you are in Parliament even if your party falls under five percent. That single rule has decided governments.

  3. 03

    Five percent, or one seat

    A party needs five percent of the party vote, or one electorate seat, to be in the room at all. Below both lines every vote cast for it is worth nothing when the seats are allocated.

  4. 04

    Sixty-one seats to govern

    A majority is 61 of 120. Under MMP that majority is almost never held by one party, which is why the real contest is the negotiation that starts the morning after the count.
